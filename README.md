# Abyrint AS Investigation: Aid Money, Profits, and Political Connections

**Compiled from public sources, company registries, media reports (Document.no, Panorama Nyheter, etc.), and official records as of November 2026.**

This is a summary of publicly available information regarding the Norwegian consultancy Abyrint AS and its owners' connections to Norwegian government and aid institutions. All claims are based on reported media coverage and public company data. No allegations of illegality are made; the focus is on transparency, high profit margins on aid-funded work, and potential conflicts of interest.

## Contracts and Work in Somalia

- **2013–2015**: Direct contracts with Norwegian Ministry of Foreign Affairs (UD). Initial large contract transferred from PwC (who withdrew due to high risk in Somalia) **without competitive tender**. Criticized by procurement experts; UD defended based on internal legal advice.
- **From 2015**: Monitoring agent and advisory roles for World Bank's Somalia Multi-Partner Fund (MPF). Tasks include fiduciary monitoring, systems for salary payments, controlling "ghost workers", institutional analysis, financial infrastructure.
- Norway has contributed hundreds of millions (e.g., ~890M since 2015 to MPF; total Norwegian aid to Somalia estimated 5–8 billion NOK over the period).
- Independent reviews (e.g., 2019 MPF mid-term) assessed monitoring function as effective and value for money in the context.
- Abyrint claims contracts won via international competitive procurement by multilateral institutions. Strand has defended high profits as compensation for specialized, high-risk work supporting a large World Bank portfolio (~75 billion NOK).

## Criticisms and Responses

**Media coverage**:
- Panorama Nyheter: Extensive series since ~2015 on high profits. Later highlighted political connections.
- Document.no (Dec 2025): "Ellevill klassereise med bistandspenger" – detailed the class journey from modest apartment in Gamle Oslo to large house in Nordstrand, wealth accumulation.
- Others: Finansavisen, Subjekt, political commentators questioning consultant share of aid and oversight.

**Expert views**:
- High profits called "embarrassing" and damaging to legitimacy of Norwegian aid (e.g., former Norad officials).
- Questions about conflicts of interest due to spouses in government/Norad.
- Calls for greater transparency and review of consultant use in aid.

**Official responses**:
- UD has raised the high profits with the World Bank.
- Development Minister has questioned consultant profits in Parliament.
- World Bank reportedly not particularly concerned.
- Strand: Role is building robust control environments; specialized boutique model with senior experts; international competition; not distributing aid but providing technical services.

**Other notes**:
- Family move: From Nonnegata 17 (Gamle Oslo, higher immigrant school) to Nordstrand (lower minority share, more affluent).
- Tax lists show significant personal wealth for Strand.
- Company has published annual accounts and auditor reports on its website.

## Conclusion

Abyrint demonstrates how specialized consultancies can generate substantial private returns from aid-funded work in fragile states. The combination of early non-competitive Norwegian contracts, sustained high margins, and marital ties to senior figures in the Norwegian aid/political ecosystem raises legitimate questions about transparency, value for money, and appearance of conflicts of interest. Whether the specialized services delivered justify the returns is a matter for donors, the World Bank, and public scrutiny. Further independent review of consultant contracting practices in Norwegian and multilateral aid would increase accountability.
